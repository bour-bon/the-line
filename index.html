Nobody meters AI in real time. These counters spread the latest annual estimates evenly over the year: 155 TWh of electricity in AI-focused data centres in 2025 (IEA, April 2026), 312–765 billion litres of water and 33–80 Mt of CO₂ for AI systems in 2025 (de Vries-Gao, Patterns), and about $730 billion in 2026 capex guidance after the July 2026 earnings. Electricity use in AI-focused data centres grew about 50% in 2025, so this year's real numbers are higher.

Money

$28 to $1

Four companies will spend $28 on data centres for every $1 of international public money that helps poor countries survive climate change.

Into AIFor climate adaptation in developing countries

Adaptation means sea walls, drought-proof crops, early-warning systems. UNEP says developing countries need at least $310 billion a year by 2035 and received $26 billion in 2023, down from $28 billion the year before. Big Tech's 2026 data-centre budget alone could close that gap twice over.

Energy

Doubling by 2030

Your chatbot answers use about 2% of the power in AI data centres. The other 98% goes on things nobody discloses.

Data centre electricity, TWh

AI-focused data centresAll other data centres

Data centres used about 1.5% of the world's electricity in 2025. The IEA expects 3% by 2030, with AI taking half. About 60% of that power still comes from coal, gas and oil.

Where AI's 155 TWh goes

Text chatbot queries ~2%Undisclosed ~98%

Analysis of IEA figures finds text queries explain about 2% of the electricity used by AI-focused data centres. Training, recommendation engines, ad targeting, image and video generation, and corporate workloads make up the rest. No company publishes that split.

Water & carbon

A New York of smoke

AI systems emitted about as much CO₂ in 2025 as New York City, and used as much water as the world drinks in bottled water.

Water from AI systems, 2025

Estimate: 312.5–764.6 billion litres

Most of it is indirect: water evaporated at the power plants feeding the servers. Companies usually report only what their own cooling towers use; the study finds indirect water use is probably three to four times higher than official estimates. Two-thirds of new US data centres built since 2022 sit in water-stressed regions.

In their own words

Every Big Tech company that reported this year said the same thing: AI pushed their emissions to records.

The case for the defence

The IEA calculates that if existing AI tools were widely adopted in energy, industry and transport, they could cut about 1,400 Mt of CO₂ a year by 2035, around five times what data centres would emit. The same report says that is about 5% of energy emissions, far short of what climate targets need, and that it depends on adoption nobody has guaranteed. Microsoft also reports replenishing more water than it withdrew in its last fiscal year, for the first time.

IEA, Energy and AI: AI and climate change · Microsoft 2026 report, via The Register
